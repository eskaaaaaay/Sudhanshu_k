Chapter 02 • Methodology

From noise and confusion to quiet clarity.

Complex challenges in organisations rarely fail because of a lack of technology. They fail because people rush into solutions before understanding what is actually happening. My approach takes unclear problems, listens for the real signal, grounds the facts in evidence, and builds practical tools that people can rely on.

01 Listen

Listen without judgment

When a system produces poor results, teams often blame human error or implement hasty patches. I begin by setting aside assumptions, observing actual workflows, and listening to the people experiencing the friction firsthand.

Real world example: When crisis chat survey scores dipped, others assumed volunteers were underperforming. Qualitative review showed the true cause: sensitive trauma questions were being asked immediately after emotional distress, overwhelming young people.
02 Declutter

Strip away the noise

Cluttered thinking creates cluttered data. I remove vanity indicators, redundant steps, and emotional friction so the core question becomes visible to all stakeholders.

Real world example: Cut through repetitive board requests by replacing ad hoc spreadsheets with a structured three tier model, giving trustees instant visibility without endless preparation back and forth.
03 Evidence

Find the grounded evidence

Empathy identifies the problem, but evidence must validate the direction. I construct clean data pipelines, model patterns, and gather empirical facts to verify whether assumptions hold up to scrutiny.

Real world example: Synthesized five years of frontline service data across 250,000 support conversations to uncover longitudinal trends in youth loneliness and peak service demand for Ireland.
04 Build

Build practical, sustainable solutions

I build tools that real people can understand and operate. Whether crafting an open public utility, an executive reporting suite, or an automated workflow, simplicity and human dignity guide every line.

Real world example: When learner driving routes were paywalled in Dublin, I gathered public records, mapped each route myself, and built an open website so anyone can practice for free.
05 Verify

Check what changed afterwards

Work does not end when code is deployed or a report is delivered. I track user adoption, evaluate subsequent performance, and ensure the original friction has actually been alleviated.

Real world example: Monitored the survey redesign over time to verify that the initial study shows an increased response rate along with higher response volume while protecting vulnerable users.
