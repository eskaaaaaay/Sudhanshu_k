Chapter 01 • The Art of Listening

Empathy is not a soft skill.
It is my foundation.

Before building data pipelines or managing digital projects, I learned how to listen. Through 2,397 conversations as a volunteer peer listener on 7Cups, I saw firsthand that people in distress rarely need quick advice. They need a quiet, non-judgmental space to be heard.

"We often hold the answers to what troubles us, but when the mind is cluttered by its own noise, those answers stay hidden. Speaking freely with someone who listens without judgment clears the clutter and brings quiet clarity."
Personal ideology

How It Began: From Member to Listener

In July 2017, experiencing firsthand how difficult it is to find non-judgmental support, I joined 7Cups as an anonymous member. The relief of being genuinely heard without fear of judgment was profound—so much so that the very next day, I registered as a volunteer peer listener.

Over the next four years, I completed 73 structured training modules covering active listening, emotional de-escalation, and online safety. Across 2,397 one-on-one chats, I supported members working through grief, anxiety, and isolation, and later joined the community safety patrol to help protect vulnerable participants from harassment.

That experience fundamentally shaped how I approach complex problems. Whether working through operational friction or technical challenges, the most effective first step is never to rush in with noisy assumptions. It is to listen without bias, clear away the clutter, and understand what is actually being experienced.
